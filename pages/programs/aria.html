ARIA

Arboviral Infection Cohort Study

About ARIA

ARIA is an international observational cohort study designed to improve understanding of arboviral infections such as dengue, Zika, and chikungunya. Conducted across primary, secondary, and tertiary healthcare facilities in multiple countries, ARIA brings together clinicians and researchers to generate real-world evidence that can improve patient care and public health responses.

The study follows patients with suspected or confirmed arboviral infections during the first 10 days of illness, collecting information on how infections are diagnosed, how patients present clinically, how severe disease develops, and how patients are managed in different healthcare settings.

A flexible, global approach

ARIA uses a tiered data collection model that allows healthcare facilities with different levels of resources and research capacity to participate:

Tier 1

Collects essential information on diagnosis, symptoms, severe disease, outcomes, and clinical management.

Tier 2

Includes additional data to address further research questions and local priorities.

Tier 3

Includes all Tier 1 and 2 data, plus biological sample collection (such as blood) to support advanced scientific investigations.

This flexible design enables broad international participation while strengthening surveillance and evidence generation across diverse healthcare systems.

What ARIA aims to learn

ARIA seeks to answer key questions about arboviral diseases, including:

  • How many suspected cases are confirmed by laboratory testing?
  • Which diagnostic tests are being used in different settings?
  • What symptoms and clinical features are most common?
  • How often do patients develop severe disease and what warning signs predict deterioration?
  • What treatments and supportive care strategies are associated with better outcomes?
  • How can clinical management be improved, particularly for patients with severe illness?

The study will also explore factors such as co-infections, hospitalisation rates, intensive care admissions, recovery, and mortality.

Who can participate?

Patients may be enrolled if they:

  • Have suspected or laboratory-confirmed arboviral infection,
  • Present to a participating healthcare facility within 7 days of symptom onset, and
  • Provide informed consent (or assent and parental/guardian consent where applicable).

All eligible suspected cases can be enrolled, while confirmed cases are followed through the acute phase of illness to assess outcomes.

Why ARIA matters

Arboviral diseases continue to place a growing burden on health systems worldwide, yet many questions remain about their diagnosis, progression, and optimal management. By collecting standardised data across countries and healthcare settings, ARIA aims to build a stronger evidence base to support clinical decision-making, identify opportunities to improve patient outcomes, and strengthen preparedness for future arboviral outbreaks.

Join ARIA

ARIA is an open, international collaboration that welcomes healthcare facilities, researchers, public health practitioners, and laboratory partners interested in advancing knowledge of arboviral diseases.

By joining ARIA, collaborators can:

  • Contribute to a global effort to improve understanding of dengue, Zika, chikungunya, and other arboviral infections.
  • Participate at a level that matches local capacity through the flexible Tier 1-3 study design.
  • Generate data relevant to local, national, and regional priorities.
  • Collaborate with an international network of clinicians, researchers, and public health experts.
  • Contribute to evidence that can improve patient care and inform public health policy.
  • Develop additional country-specific or regional research questions alongside the core study objectives.
